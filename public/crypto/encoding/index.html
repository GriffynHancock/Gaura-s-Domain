Crypto 101 · Module 02 · Encoding

Encoding is not encryption

Encoding is used every day to move information around by changing its form while preserving its contents. It's not about actually making it secret — though it is used to hide malicious information from automated detection. Learning to recognise the difference between encoding methods is very important in cybersecurity.

ℹ The most common forms of encoding are base64, hexadecimal and URL encoding. Encoding methods turn information from one standard into another, and each standard has a fixed set of characters available to it. Base64 can be recognised because it uses uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and the symbols + and / — and it's often (not always) padded with one or two = signs on the end. dGhpcw== is base64 for “this” — see the == on the end? That padding is usually the fastest tell you've got. Hexadecimal only uses the numbers 0–9 and the letters a–f, and is usually lowercase. URL encoding uses lots of % signs paired with two hex digits (0–9 and A–F) to stand in for symbols and spaces — %20 is a space, %2F is a slash.
